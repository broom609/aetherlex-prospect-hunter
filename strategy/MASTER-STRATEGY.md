## 1. Niche selection

### What makes a niche good for lead-gen

You are looking for the intersection of **high lead value**, **urgency/emergency
intent**, **healthy search volume**, and **easy fulfillment** (the business can
service a lead without you needing deep technical knowledge). The ranking factors:

| Factor | Why it matters | What "good" looks like |
|---|---|---|
| **Lead value** | Determines what a buyer will pay per lead | $50–$500+ per qualified lead; job value $1k–$30k |
| **Urgency** | Urgent jobs convert fast and tolerate "shared" leads | Emergency / time-sensitive (water in basement *now*) |
| **Search volume** | You need enough "[service] [city]" demand | 100+ local monthly searches per metro for the head term |
| **Competition** | Weak local SEO = faster ranking | Few optimized sites; thin GBP profiles; no schema |
| **Monetization** | How many ways you can sell and how high the ceiling is | High job value + repeat + recurring potential |
| **Ease of fulfillment** | How simple it is to qualify and route a lead | Clear yes/no qualifiers; standard service area |

### Ranked tiers

**Tier S — start here (highest lead value + urgency + recurring upsell)**

| Niche | Typical lead value | Why it wins | Watch-outs |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Water damage / restoration** | $75–$300+ | Extreme urgency, insurance pays, high job value | Buyers must answer 24/7 |
| **Mold remediation** | $75–$250 | Urgent, health-driven, high ticket, weak competition | Seasonal + regional |
| **Roofing (repair + replace)** | $50–$200 | Huge job value, storm-driven spikes | Competitive in big metros |
| **Foundation / waterproofing** | $75–$250 | High ticket, fear-driven, weak SEO competition | Longer sales cycle |

**Tier A — excellent, slightly more competition**

| Niche | Lead value | Notes |
|---|---|---|
| **HVAC (repair + install)** | $40–$150 | Year-round, emergency + replacement, strong recurring (maintenance) |
| **Plumbing** | $35–$150 | Constant demand, emergency intent, easy to qualify |
| **Tree service / removal** | $30–$120 | Storm-driven, high ticket, fragmented competitors |
| **Junk removal** | $20–$60 | High volume, fast close, very easy fulfillment, great starter |
| **Pest control** | $25–$80 | Recurring contracts, year-round, weak local SEO in suburbs |

**Tier B — good, often longer cycles or lower urgency**

| Niche | Lead value | Notes |
|---|---|---|
| **Concrete / driveways** | $30–$100 | High ticket, seasonal, project-based |
| **Fencing** | $30–$100 | Project-based, good ticket, low urgency |
| **Landscaping / hardscaping** | $20–$80 | Recurring (maintenance) + project (hardscape) |
| **Garage doors** | $25–$80 | Repair = urgent; install = project |
| **Window/door replacement** | $40–$150 | High ticket, longer cycle |

**Tier C — high value but harder to fulfill/regulate**

| Niche | Lead value | Notes |
|---|---|---|
| **Med spas / aesthetics** | $30–$150 | High LTV but medical-adjacent compliance; appointment-based |
| **Dentists / implants / ortho** | $50–$300 | Very high LTV but heavy competition + medical compliance |
| **Personal injury / legal** | $200–$1,000+ | Highest value, brutal competition + bar advertising rules |
| **Solar** | $50–$250 | High ticket but volatile incentives/regulation |

### Recommendation

**Start with one Tier S or Tier A niche where competition in your chosen metro is
visibly weak.** For a first asset with low risk and fast feedback, the best
combination of urgency + easy fulfillment + fast close is **water damage
restoration** (highest value/urgency) or **junk removal** (easiest to validate and
close). If you want maximum upside and can tolerate buyers who must answer 24/7,
pick **water damage / restoration** or **roofing**. If you want the fastest proof
of concept, pick **junk removal** or **HVAC**.

> **Starter pick used throughout this doc: Water Damage Restoration**, with
> **Junk Removal** as the "easy-mode" alternative. Swap freely — the system is
> niche-agnostic.

## 2. Market selection

You want **markets where lead value is high but local SEO competition is weak.**
The biggest mistake is launching in the largest, most competitive city in your
region. Instead, target **affluent suburbs, secondary cities, and county
service-area clusters** around a metro.

### The scoring framework

Score each candidate city/suburb 1–5 on each factor; weight and sum.

| Factor | Weight | Data source | What you want |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Lead value potential** (income + home value) | 25% | Census / Zillow | High median home value & income |
| **Homeownership rate** | 15% | Census ACS | 60%+ owner-occupied (homeowners buy services) |
| **Search demand** | 20% | Keyword tools / Google autocomplete | Measurable "[service] [city]" volume |
| **Competition weakness** | 25% | Manual SERP + GBP audit | Few optimized sites, thin GBPs, no schema |
| **Business density** (buyers) | 15% | Google Maps count | Enough providers to sell to (5–30 is ideal) |

**Competition weakness is the single best lever** — it's where you find
"underpriced" rankings. Audit it manually (cheap and accurate):

1. Search `[service] [city]` and `[service] near me` from that location (use a VPN
   or Google's location override).
2. Look at the **top 5 organic results**. Are they thin, national directories
   (Yelp, Angi, Thumbtack), or dated small-business sites? Directories ranking =
   opportunity. Optimized local sites with reviews + schema = harder.
3. Look at the **map pack**. Few listings, low review counts (<30), incomplete
   profiles = weak.
4. Check whether anyone owns **"[service] [city]" as an exact-match content page**.
   Usually nobody does in suburbs — that's your wedge.

### Market-selection playbook

1. Pick a **metro hub** you understand (e.g., Philadelphia).
2. List the **15–25 surrounding suburbs/towns** (e.g., Cherry Hill, Marlton,
   Voorhees, Moorestown, Medford, Mount Laurel, Haddonfield, Washington Twp,
   Sewell, Glassboro for South Jersey).
3. Pull **median home value, median household income, homeownership %, population**
   for each (Census QuickFacts is free).
4. Run the **manual SERP + map-pack audit** for your niche in each.
5. Score, rank, and pick **3–5 anchor cities** to launch first, plus a tail of
   10–15 smaller surrounding towns for programmatic city pages.

**Rule of thumb:** affluent suburb + high homeownership + national directories
ranking on page one + thin map pack = launch here first.

## 3. Website architecture

### Core principle: topical silos + clean URLs + strong internal linking

The site is organized as **service silos** crossed with **location pages**, all
feeding the **money pages** (city + service-in-city pages with the lead form).

### Site map

```
/                                   Homepage (brand + primary service + primary city)
/services/                          Services hub (lists all services)
  /services/water-damage-restoration/
  /services/mold-remediation/
  /services/basement-flood-cleanup/
  /services/sewage-cleanup/
/locations/                         Locations hub (lists all served cities)
  /locations/cherry-hill-nj/        City page (all services in that city)
  /locations/marlton-nj/
  /locations/voorhees-nj/
/[service]-[city]/  (money pages)   Service-in-city pages (highest commercial intent)
  /water-damage-restoration-cherry-hill-nj/
  /water-damage-restoration-marlton-nj/
/areas/                             Neighborhood / service-area pages (optional, deeper)
  /areas/cherry-hill/barclay/
/resources/  or  /blog/             Informational / AEO / GEO content
  /resources/how-much-does-water-damage-restoration-cost/
  /resources/what-to-do-when-your-basement-floods/
/compare/                           Comparison pages
  /compare/water-damage-restoration-vs-mold-remediation/
  /compare/diy-vs-professional-water-extraction/
/faq/                               FAQ hub (+ FAQ blocks on every money page)
/about/                             Trust / E-E-A-T
/reviews/                           Aggregated reviews + Review schema
/contact/                           Contact + quote form
/get-a-quote/  or  /free-estimate/  Primary conversion page
/thank-you/                         Post-conversion (tracking + next steps)
```

### URL rules

- **Lowercase, hyphenated, keyword-first, no dates, no parameters.**
- Money pages use the **exact commercial phrase**: `/water-damage-restoration-cherry-hill-nj/`.
  This wins the "[service] in [city]" query and reads cleanly to AI engines.
- Keep depth shallow (≤3 levels). Every money page reachable within 2 clicks of home.
- One H1 per page, matching search intent.

### Navigation & internal linking

- **Header nav:** Services (dropdown), Service Areas (dropdown), Reviews, About,
  a phone number (click-to-call, always visible), and a CTA button ("Free Estimate").
- **Footer:** full list of cities + services (crawlable, distributes link equity).
- **Silo linking rules:**
  - Service hub → each service page → each service-in-city money page.
  - City page → every service offered in that city, and → neighboring city pages.
  - Money page → parent service page, parent city page, related FAQ/resource posts.
  - Resource/blog posts → relevant money pages with descriptive anchor text
    (e.g., "professional [water damage restoration in Cherry Hill]").
- **Breadcrumbs** on every page (Home › Locations › Cherry Hill › Water Damage),
  reinforced with `BreadcrumbList` schema.

### Content-silo logic

Each **service** is a silo. The service hub page is the pillar; service pages are
sub-pillars; service-in-city money pages and supporting resource posts are the
leaves. This concentrates topical authority and tells Google (and AI crawlers)
exactly what each cluster is about.

## 4. SEO content strategy

### Keyword map by intent

| Intent | Pattern | Example | Target page |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Commercial (head)** | `[service] [city]` | water damage restoration cherry hill | Money page |
| **Commercial (near me)** | `[service] near me` | water damage restoration near me | Home + money pages + GBP |
| **Commercial (best)** | `best [service] in [city]` | best water damage company cherry hill | Money page / reviews page |
| **Emergency** | `emergency [service] [city]` | emergency water removal marlton | Money page (emergency block) |
| **Cost** | `[service] cost in [city]` | water damage restoration cost nj | Resource/cost page |
| **Problem/repair** | `[problem] repair [city]` | basement flood cleanup voorhees | Service / money page |
| **Question (AEO)** | `how/what/why/does [topic]` | how long does water damage drying take | Resource + FAQ |
| **Comparison** | `[a] vs [b]` | mold removal vs remediation | Comparison page |

### Keyword tiers per money page

For each `/[service]-[city]/` page:

- **Primary (1):** `[service] [city]` (e.g., "water damage restoration Cherry Hill NJ").
- **Secondary (3–6):** `emergency [service] [city]`, `[service] company [city]`,
  `24 hour [service] [city]`, `[service] near me`, `[problem] cleanup [city]`.
- **Long-tail (8–15):** `flooded basement cleanup Cherry Hill`, `burst pipe water
  damage Cherry Hill NJ`, `water extraction services near Barclay Cherry Hill`, etc.
- **Question keywords (for FAQ block):** "How much does water damage restoration
  cost in NJ?", "Does insurance cover water damage in New Jersey?", "How fast can
  you respond?"
- **Comparison keywords:** handled on dedicated `/compare/` pages, linked in.

### Volume strategy

In suburbs, exact "[service] [city]" volume is often low (10–50/mo) but **extremely
high intent and low competition**. Win by **aggregating many city pages** + the
broad "near me" + map-pack visibility. Don't chase national head terms; own the
local long tail at scale.

### Content depth standard

- Money pages: **800–1,500 words**, genuinely useful, locally specific (local
  landmarks, neighborhoods, climate/soil/weather factors, local code/insurance
  notes), with a direct-answer intro, service details, process, pricing guidance,
  trust signals, FAQ block, and CTA.
- Resource posts: **1,200–2,500 words**, answer-first, comprehensive.

## 5. AEO / GEO content strategy

**AEO** = win featured snippets, voice answers, "People Also Ask," and direct
answers. **GEO** = get cited and recommended inside ChatGPT, Perplexity, Gemini,
Copilot, and Google AI Overviews. Both reward the same things: **clear, factual,
well-structured, entity-rich, extractable content.**

### The format patterns AI engines extract

1. **Direct-answer block (the snippet magnet).** Open the section with the
   question as an H2/H3, then answer in **40–55 words** in the first sentence,
   factual and self-contained, before expanding. Example:
   > **How much does water damage restoration cost in New Jersey?**
   > Water damage restoration in New Jersey typically costs **$1,300–$5,600**,
   > averaging around **$3,800**. Minor cleanups start near $450; major jobs
   > involving mold or structural drying can exceed $10,000. Final cost depends on
   > water category, affected square footage, and how quickly mitigation begins.

2. **Definition blocks.** Define the service/entity in one tight sentence ("Water
   damage restoration is the process of…"). AI engines lift these verbatim.

3. **Numbered steps / process lists.** "What to do when your basement floods:
   1) Shut off power. 2) Stop the source. 3) Document for insurance. 4) Call a
   certified restoration company within 24–48 hours to prevent mold." Steps map
   directly to voice answers and AI summaries.

4. **Comparison tables.** Two-column "vs." tables (cost, timeline, when to choose)
   are highly extractable and frequently quoted by AI engines.

5. **Entity-rich paragraphs.** Name real entities: certifications (IICRC),
   standards, equipment (air movers, dehumidifiers), local cities, insurance terms,
   water categories (Cat 1/2/3). Entities help AI systems understand and trust the
   page.

6. **Summary / "key takeaways" block.** A 3–5 bullet TL;DR near the top or bottom
   gives AI a pre-chewed summary to cite.

7. **FAQ blocks** with concise 2–4 sentence answers, marked up with `FAQPage`
   schema (see §6).

### GEO-specific tactics

- **Be the most quotable source:** lead with the answer, cite numbers/ranges,
  state recency ("As of 2026…"), and attribute expertise (author with credentials).
- **Consistency across the web (entity SEO):** identical NAP (Name, Address, Phone)
  everywhere, consistent brand entity, links from local sources. AI engines
  triangulate trust from corroboration.
- **Structured data everywhere** (it's a primary signal AI crawlers parse).
- **Stat-and-source style:** sentences like "According to [authority], X" tend to
  get reused. Include original, specific local data where you can (response times,
  local pricing ranges) — uniqueness gets cited.
- **Crawlability for AI bots:** allow `GPTBot`, `OAI-SearchBot`, `PerplexityBot`,
  `Google-Extended`, `ClaudeBot` in `robots.txt` (your choice — allowing them is
  how you get cited; see §15).
- **Clean, semantic HTML:** real `<h1>`–`<h3>`, lists, tables, `<article>`; avoid
  burying answers in JavaScript-only rendering.

### Page-level AEO/GEO checklist

- [ ] H1 matches the query intent.
- [ ] First 55 words directly answer the primary question.
- [ ] At least one definition, one list/steps, one table.
- [ ] FAQ block (3–6 Q&As) + `FAQPage` schema.
- [ ] "Key takeaways" summary block.
- [ ] Named entities (certs, cities, standards, terms).
- [ ] Author/reviewer with credentials + last-updated date.
- [ ] Valid `LocalBusiness` / `Service` / `FAQPage` JSON-LD.

## 6. Schema markup plan

Use **JSON-LD**, one block per page, validated with Google's Rich Results Test and
Schema.org validator. Full copy-paste examples are in
`templates/schema-markup.md`. Summary of what goes where:

| Schema type | Where | Key properties to include |
|---|---|---|
| **Organization** | Site-wide (home) | name, url, logo, sameAs (social), contactPoint |
| **LocalBusiness** (or subtype, e.g. `HomeAndConstructionBusiness`) | Home + city + money pages | name, image, address, geo, telephone, areaServed, openingHours, priceRange, aggregateRating |
| **Service** | Service + money pages | serviceType, provider, areaServed, description, offers |
| **FAQPage** | Any page with FAQ block | question + acceptedAnswer pairs (must match visible content) |
| **Review** + **AggregateRating** | Reviews page, money pages | author, reviewRating, reviewBody; ratingValue, reviewCount |
| **BreadcrumbList** | Every deep page | itemListElement with position/name/item |
| **WebPage** / **WebSite** | Site-wide | name, url, potentialAction (SearchAction) |
| **Offer / Product** | If selling a defined package | price/priceCurrency or priceSpecification, availability |

**Critical compliance rule:** schema must reflect **real, on-page, truthful**
information. Don't put `aggregateRating` you can't substantiate or a `LocalBusiness`
address you don't operate from — both can trigger manual actions and (for reviews)
FTC issues. See §15.

## 7. Lead capture system

### The funnel

```
Traffic (organic + map pack + AI citations)
        │
        ▼
 Money page / city page  ── sticky click-to-call + form above the fold
        │
        ├── Phone call  ──► tracked number (CallRail) ──► rings buyer or your line
        │
        └── Form submit ──► instant qualification ──► CRM ──► routed to buyer
        │
        ▼
 Thank-you page (tracking fires) + missed-call text-back + booking link
        │
        ▼
 Qualified lead delivered to buyer (SMS + email + CRM, < 2 min)
```

### Capture mechanisms (use all)

- **Sticky click-to-call** header/footer bar on mobile (most local leads are phone).
- **Call tracking** with a dedicated tracked number per site (and per channel if
  you scale). Records, transcribes, and timestamps every call for quality proof.
- **Short multi-step quote form** above the fold (see `templates/lead-capture-form.md`).
- **Urgency-based CTAs:** "24/7 Emergency Response — Call Now," "We can be there
  in 60 minutes," countdown/availability framing where truthful.
- **Live chat / chatbot** that captures name + phone + problem and routes instantly.
- **Missed-call text-back:** if a call isn't answered, auto-SMS within seconds
  ("Sorry we missed you — reply here and we'll dispatch help"). This recovers
  20–40% of otherwise-lost leads.
- **Booking form / calendar** for non-emergency services (med spa, fencing quotes).
- **Exit-intent / scroll CTA** with a secondary offer (free inspection).

### Lead qualification (before you sell)

Qualify on capture so you only sell good leads:

- **Service match:** is the request a service the buyer actually offers?
- **Location match:** inside the buyer's service area / ZIP list?
- **Intent/urgency:** "need it now" vs "just researching."
- **Validity:** real phone (validated), not spam, business hours / 24-7 flag.
- **Job-type filters:** e.g., for water damage — residential vs commercial,
  insurance vs out-of-pocket, standing water yes/no.

Score each lead (e.g., 0–100) from form answers + call transcript keywords. Only
deliver leads above your threshold; log the rest. This is what lets you sell
"qualified leads," not raw clicks (see §13).

### Lead routing

- **Exclusive model:** route to one buyer instantly (SMS + email + CRM + optional
  warm phone transfer).
- **Shared model:** route the same lead to 2–4 buyers simultaneously (speed-to-lead
  contest). Cap the number sold.
- **Round-robin / geo-routing:** route by ZIP to the buyer who owns that area.

## 8. Monetization model

| Model | How it works | Pros | Cons | Best for |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| **Pay-per-lead (PPL)** | Charge per qualified lead ($X each) | Easy to start, scales with volume, low buyer risk | You eat slow months; disputes over quality | **Starting out** |
| **Exclusive leads** | One buyer gets the lead, premium price | Highest per-lead price, easy to sell | Lower volume per buyer | High-ticket niches (restoration, roofing) |
| **Shared leads** | Sell same lead to 2–4 buyers | More revenue per lead, fills volume | Buyers dislike competition; quality complaints | High-urgency, high-volume niches |
| **Monthly retainer** | Flat fee for all leads in an area | Predictable MRR, sticky | Must prove value monthly; you carry volume risk | After you've proven volume |
| **Commission / referral** | % of closed-job revenue | Aligns incentives, big upside | Hard to track/enforce; trust required | Trusted buyer relationships |
| **Rank-and-rent** | Rent the whole ranked site to one business | High MRR, simple, you keep the asset | Need the site ranking first | The end-game per market |

### Recommended path (low risk → scale)

1. **Start: Pay-per-lead, exclusive, with the first buyer on a no-risk trial**
   ("first 5 leads free, then $X each, cancel anytime"). Removes buyer risk, gets
   you proof and testimonials fast.
2. **Then: move winners to a monthly retainer or rank-and-rent** once you can show
   consistent volume — this converts variable PPL into predictable MRR and is the
   most valuable, lowest-maintenance state.
3. **Use shared leads only in high-volume, high-urgency niches** where buyers
   accept competition (and price the lead lower).
4. **Rank-and-rent is the asset goal:** one ranked site = one rent check, minimal
   ongoing work, and a sellable asset (sites sell for 20–40× monthly rent).

> **Pricing anchor:** price per lead at **8–15% of the buyer's average job value**,
> or set rank-and-rent rent at **~10–20% of the monthly revenue the leads generate.**
> Validate against what they currently pay Angi/Thumbtack/Google LSA per lead.

## 9. Tech stack

Prioritized for **low cost + scalability**. Two viable paths:

### Path A — "Marketing OS in a box" (fastest, all-in-one)

- **GoHighLevel (GHL):** sites/funnels, forms, CRM, pipelines, SMS/email,
  missed-call text-back, calendars, reputation/reviews, reporting — all in one
  (~$97–$297/mo, agency plan resells unlimited sub-accounts). **Best for scaling a
  network** because each city/niche site is a sub-account.

### Path B — "Best-of-breed" (more control, can be cheaper at 1 site)

| Layer | Tool(s) | Notes |
|---|---|---|
| **CMS / builder** | WordPress + Astra/GeneratePress; or Webflow; or Next.js (Astro) on Vercel | WP = best for programmatic local pages at scale + plugins |
| **Hosting** | Cloudflare Pages / Vercel (static) or Cloudways/Kinsta (WP) | Fast Core Web Vitals; Cloudflare CDN |
| **Programmatic pages** | WP + custom fields (ACF) or a static-site generator | Generate city/service pages from a data table |
| **Analytics** | GA4 + Google Search Console + Microsoft Clarity (free heatmaps) | Clarity = free session recordings |
| **Call tracking** | CallRail (or GHL native) | Dynamic number insertion, recording, transcription |
| **CRM** | GoHighLevel / HubSpot (free tier) / Airtable | Airtable is a great cheap lead DB |
| **Forms** | GHL forms / Fillout / Typeform / native | Multi-step, conditional logic |
| **Automation** | Make (cheap), n8n (self-host, cheapest at scale), Zapier (easiest) | Make/n8n best price-to-power |
| **AI content** | Claude / GPT for drafting + a human edit pass; SurferSEO/Frase optional | Never publish unedited (see §11/§15) |
| **Keyword research** | Ahrefs / Semrush (or budget: Keywords Everywhere, Google autocomplete, Search Console) | Start cheap; add Ahrefs when revenue justifies |
| **Rank tracking** | AccuRanker / Nightwatch / SerpRobot (local grid: Local Falcon) | Local Falcon for map-pack grid tracking |
| **Reporting** | Looker Studio (free, connects GA4/GSC/CallRail/Sheets) | Client-facing dashboards |
| **Payments/billing** | Stripe | Subscriptions for retainers, invoices for PPL |
| **SMS/voice** | Twilio (or GHL native); Retell/Vapi for AI voice answering | AI receptionist to qualify/route 24/7 |

**Recommended starter stack (lean, ~$150–$300/mo):** WordPress + GeneratePress on
Cloudways **or** GoHighLevel agency plan, CallRail (or GHL calls), Airtable/GHL CRM,
Make or n8n for automation, GA4 + GSC + Clarity + Looker Studio (all free),
Local Falcon for map tracking.

## 10. Automation plan

Full workflow with node-by-node steps is in `templates/automation-workflow.md`.
The core automations:

1. **Lead intake → score → route (the money automation).**
   `Form/Call webhook → validate phone → score lead → branch (qualified?) →
   create CRM record → notify buyer (SMS + email) → log to Sheet/Airtable → start
   follow-up sequence.` Target speed-to-lead: **under 2 minutes.**

2. **Missed-call text-back.** `Call ends unanswered → wait 30s → send SMS to caller
   → create lead → alert you/buyer.`

3. **Lead scoring.** Rules or AI: parse form fields + call transcript for
   service/location/urgency keywords → assign 0–100 → tag tier.

4. **Buyer notification & delivery.** Instant SMS + email with lead details +
   recording link; for exclusive leads, optional warm transfer; daily digest of
   all leads.

5. **CRM updates & status tracking.** Lead moves through pipeline stages
   (New → Delivered → Contacted → Quoted → Won/Lost); buyer marks outcome via a
   reply link (feeds quality reporting).

6. **Follow-up sequences.** Automated nurture to the *lead* (if you own the
   relationship) and to the *buyer* (reminders to act fast, request outcome).

7. **Reporting refresh.** Nightly: push call/lead/outcome data to Sheets/Airtable →
   Looker Studio dashboard updates automatically.

8. **Client billing.**
   - **PPL:** monthly tally of qualified leads × price → Stripe invoice (auto).
   - **Retainer/rank-and-rent:** Stripe subscription (auto-charges monthly).
   - Dunning + receipt emails automated.

**Tooling:** Make or n8n as the spine (webhooks from forms/CallRail/GHL), Twilio for
SMS, Airtable/Sheets as the data store, Stripe for billing, Retell/Vapi if you add
an AI voice agent that answers and qualifies calls 24/7.

## 11. Content production workflow

Goal: **publish unique, useful, locally-relevant pages at scale — without spam.**
The line between "programmatic SEO" (good) and "doorway pages" (penalized) is
**genuine local value and meaningful differentiation per page.**

### The repeatable pipeline

1. **Data layer (the spreadsheet).** One row per city, with columns: city, county,
   ZIP(s), population, neighborhoods, nearest hospital/landmarks, local risk factors
   (flood zones, soil, weather), local pricing range, response time, 1–2 unique
   local notes, target keywords. **This local data is what makes each page unique.**
2. **Templates.** Use the service/city/FAQ/blog/comparison templates (see
   `templates/`). Templates define structure; the data layer fills uniqueness.
3. **AI draft.** Prompt Claude/GPT with the row data + template to produce a
   first draft. **Each page must use the row's unique local facts** — never just
   swap the city name (that's spam and gets filtered).
4. **Human edit pass (non-negotiable).** Fact-check pricing/claims, add a real
   local detail or photo, tighten the direct-answer block, ensure accuracy. ~15–20
   min/page. This is your quality moat and compliance safeguard.
5. **On-page SEO/AEO check.** Title, meta, H1, internal links, schema, FAQ, image
   alt text, direct-answer block, key-takeaways box.
6. **Publish + internal link** into the silo (hub → page, neighbor cities, related
   resources).
7. **Index + track.** Submit to GSC, add to sitemap, add to rank tracker + Local
   Falcon grid.

### Anti-spam rules

- **No mass-published, identical "[city] swap" pages.** Each needs ≥30% unique,
  locally-specific content and a reason to exist.
- **Stagger publishing** (e.g., 5–15 pages/week, not 500 overnight) to look natural
  and let you maintain quality.
- **Prune** pages that get no impressions after 90 days (consolidate or improve).
- **Real E-E-A-T:** named author/owner, real business entity, real reviews, real
  photos, real phone that a human (or AI agent) answers.

### What "unique + useful + local" means per page type

- **Service page:** the service explained well + your process + pricing guidance +
  certifications + FAQs.
- **City page:** the service *in that city* — neighborhoods served, local risks,
  response times from your nearest base, local landmarks, city-specific FAQs,
  local reviews.
- **FAQ page/block:** real questions people ask (mine from "People Also Ask,"
  call transcripts, GBP questions).
- **Blog/resource:** genuinely helpful, answer-first guides that earn citations and
  link internally to money pages.
- **Comparison:** honest, table-driven "X vs Y" / "DIY vs pro" decisions.

## 13. Outreach plan

**Frame everything as "qualified local leads / booked jobs," not "marketing
services."** Business owners are sick of agencies; they will always take more
*customers*. Sell the result, not the mechanism.

### The offer

> "I generate [service] leads in [city] from people actively searching right now.
> I'll send you the first [3–5] **free** so you can see the quality. After that
> it's $[X] per qualified lead — exclusive to you, no contract, cancel anytime."

Risk reversal (free trial leads + no contract) is what gets the first yes.

### Channels & sequence (multi-touch, ~6–9 touches over 2–3 weeks)

1. **Cold email** (primary, scalable) — short, result-focused, one CTA.
2. **Cold call** (highest conversion for local) — pattern-interrupt + offer.
3. **Voicemail** (when no answer) — 15-second teaser that drives a callback.
4. **SMS** (after a call/voicemail, where permitted) — soft nudge with the offer.
5. **Loom audit** (the differentiator) — 2-min screen recording showing the local
   SERP, that they're not ranking, and the leads your site is capturing.
6. **In-person / drop-by** (for high-ticket local trades) — leave a one-pager.

Full scripts for all six channels are in `templates/outreach-scripts.md`.

### Targeting

Pull 30–50 buyers from Google Maps for your niche+metro. Prioritize businesses that
are **already paying for leads** (running Google Ads, on Angi/Thumbtack, have LSA
badges) — they understand lead value and have budget. Skip the #1 organic player
(they don't need you); target the hungry #4–#15.

### Selling the first 10 leads

1. Generate a small buffer of real, qualified leads first (even 3–5).
2. Pitch 20–40 buyers; offer the **first 5 leads free, exclusive**.
3. Deliver instantly (speed-to-lead), with the call recording/transcript as proof.
4. After they close a job from your free leads, convert to **$X/qualified lead**.
5. Get the outcome ("did it turn into a job?") to compute close rate + ROI, then use
   that to raise price and recruit the next buyer (or move to retainer/rank-and-rent).

## 15. Risk & compliance

This model is legitimate and widely run, **but several common tactics are against
platform rules or the law.** Here's the honest breakdown and the safe structure.

### Real risks

- **Fake / "virtual" Google Business Profiles.** Creating GBP listings for a
  business that doesn't really operate at an address, using fake addresses, or
  keyword-stuffing the name **violates Google's guidelines** and gets listings
  suspended/banned — and can poison your domain's local trust. **Don't fabricate
  GBP listings.** Options that are safe: (a) operate a *real* service entity with a
  real address/staff you can verify, or (b) do **rank-and-rent on the organic side**
  and let the *buyer's real GBP* handle the map pack (you partner, they own the
  profile).
- **Fake business names / fake reviews.** Inventing reviews or using fake names is
  **illegal** under the FTC's rule on fake reviews/testimonials (substantial civil
  penalties) and violates Google's policies. Only display real, verifiable reviews;
  only mark up `aggregateRating` you can substantiate.
- **Local SEO spam (doorway pages).** Mass-producing near-identical city pages with
  no unique value is a Google spam violation and gets filtered/penalized. Mitigate
  with the genuine-local-value workflow in §11.
- **Call recording / tracking compliance.** Many states are **two-party (all-party)
  consent** for call recording (e.g., recording calls into CA, PA, etc.). Use a
  recording disclosure ("this call may be recorded") and configure your call-tracking
  tool's consent settings per state. For SMS, comply with **TCPA** (consent, opt-out)
  — automated/marketing texts to people who didn't opt in is a real liability.
- **Advertising claims.** "Best," "#1," "guaranteed," fake urgency, fake scarcity,
  or response-time promises you can't keep can be deceptive (FTC + state UDAP laws).
  Keep claims truthful and substantiable.
- **Lead-selling / data compliance.** Disclose in your privacy policy that submitted
  info is shared with a service provider to fulfill the request. Mind state privacy
  laws (CCPA/CPRA etc.) about selling/sharing personal data; for some verticals
  (health/med spa) be careful with sensitive data and HIPAA-adjacent issues.
- **Schema honesty.** Misleading structured data (fake ratings, wrong location) can
  trigger manual actions and rich-result penalties.

### The safe, legitimate structure

1. **Form a real legal entity** (LLC) and use a **real, answerable phone number**
   (or AI receptionist) — not a fake persona.
2. **Be transparent about the model.** You operate a lead-generation site and refer
   qualified inquiries to a vetted local provider. A clear privacy policy + terms +
   recording/SMS consent covers most exposure.
3. **For the map pack, don't fake GBPs.** Either run a genuine service business with
   verifiable presence, or partner with the buyer's real GBP and focus your asset on
   organic + AEO/GEO.
4. **Only real reviews.** Collect them legitimately (from the buyer's actual
   customers, attributed) and never fabricate ratings or schema.
5. **Truthful content.** Pricing ranges, response times, and claims must be real or
   clearly framed as estimates; cite sources.
6. **Consent everywhere.** Call-recording disclosure (configured per state),
   TCPA-compliant SMS with opt-out, GDPR/CCPA-aware privacy policy.
7. **Quality over scale.** Unique, useful, locally-relevant pages — never doorway
   spam.

> Bottom line: the rankable, sellable asset is built on **real entity + real
> reviews + truthful, locally-useful content + compliant tracking.** That's also
> what ranks best and gets cited by AI engines, so the compliant path and the
> effective path are the same path. (This is general guidance, not legal advice —
> confirm state-specific call-recording, TCPA, and advertising rules with a
> lawyer before launch.)
